N° 01 · WHAT VOIX IS

A voice interface for Home Assistant that actually has a conversation.

Voix turns the Home Assistant Voice PE into something worth talking to. Real-time conversation, optional dictation, modes for different moods. Locally hosted. Nothing leaves the network except the LLM call.

01 · WHY

The puck deserved better.

Beautiful hardware shipped with a polite default brain. Voix is the upgrade — the conversation the device was always capable of.

02 · WHO FOR

People who own the network.

Homelab tinkerers. Home Assistant owners. The kind of person who reads release notes for fun and wires their house themselves.

03 · WHAT MAKES IT VOIX

Confident, not clever.

It listens, answers, remembers. It doesn't preface. It doesn't apologise. It doesn't pretend to be a person — but it talks like one.

N° 02 · THE SYSTEM

Two registers. One brand.

The brand lives in the dialogue between two surfaces: cream paper for thought, dark for state. Same wordmark, same shape language, same HA blue — but doing different jobs in each.

PAPER · CREAM

Restrained. Considered.

Cream paper (#FAF8F3), ink black type, HA blue used only as a punchline accent on a single word or phrase. This is where ideas live: marketing copy, headlines, the README, anything still.

DARK · LIVE

Active. Listening.

Ink black ground (#18181B), pale type, HA blue carrying state. The blue earns its place — when you see it, Voix is doing something. Use for the product itself, status, anything live.

PUCK · ACTIVE · RINGS ARE THE VOICE

N° 03 · COLOUR

Three colours. That's all.

A restrained palette is the system. The discipline is what makes it work — adding a fourth colour breaks the contract between paper and dark.

Paper #FAF8F3

Hero canvas. Cream, warm, not white. Anything cooler reads as SaaS.

Ink #18181B

Primary type, dark surfaces, the puck body. Never pure black.

HA blue #03A9F4

The accent. Borrowed from Home Assistant on purpose — to signal we live in that ecosystem without copying it.

Paper warm #F4F0E6

Secondary surface. Use sparingly for cards and depth within the cream world.

DO

Let the blue be rare. One word per headline. One state per screen. Restraint is what makes it land when it does appear.

DON'T

Add a second accent colour. No amber, no coral, no purple gradients. The discipline of three colours is the brand.

N° 04 · TYPE

Instrument Serif. Hanken Grotesk. JetBrains Mono.

A serif for the wordmark and display. A grotesque for the body. A monospace for system text. All three are free, all three have character, none are on the AI-slop default list.

DON'T

N° 05 · THE SHAPE

One puck. Many sizes.

A rounded square with a centred circle. The body is the squircle, the voice is the circle. They scale together, in fixed proportion, from favicon to hero. They are never separated.

DO

Centre the circle. Always. Geometrically dead-centre, not optically. The puck is a symmetrical object.

DON'T

Don't change the proportions. Don't rotate it. Don't add a face. Don't put it next to other logos in a "as seen on" row.

N° 06 · LAYOUT

The puck sits at the bottom. The page is the room.

On hero surfaces the puck is a real-sized object at the bottom of the viewport, partly cropped. The headline floats above it. The composition is the metaphor — small device, big voice.

N° 07 · VOICE

Confident. Specific. Slightly knowing.

The voice has opinions. It's not impressed with itself, but it's not apologising for itself either. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. The cheek is in the timing, not the volume.

HEADLINE · SECTION 02

BODY · WHY IT EXISTS

Your puck running an actual brain. Locally. Quietly. With opinions.

BODY · WHAT IT IS

DO

Concrete nouns. "Pulled pork. Six hours in." Tell people exactly what's happening.

DON'T

No "harness the power of." No "seamlessly." No "intelligent" or "smart." No exclamation marks. Ever.

DO

Take the piss out of itself before anyone else does. "It came with a brain the size of a peanut. We swapped it."

DON'T

Punch down at the user. No "finally a voice assistant that doesn't suck." Cheek is at the category, not the person.

N° 08 · WORDMARK

The glyph. The name. The pronunciation.

Three elements, always in this order, always together. The pronunciation glyph /vwa/ is part of the wordmark. It tells people how to say it and signals that this is French, on purpose.

GLYPH · WORDMARK · PRONUNCIATION

DO

Always include the pronunciation glyph next to the wordmark in primary use. It's not optional.

DON'T

Don't write "voix (pronounced 'vwa')" in body copy. The glyph does that work, once, in the wordmark.

N° 09 · NEVER

The things that would kill it.

Slop is the enemy. These are the moves that look fine in isolation and ruin the brand in aggregate. They are easier to do than the right thing, which is why the rule has to be explicit.

NEVER

Em dashes in marketing copy. Use hyphens or full stops. Em dashes are an AI tell.

NEVER

Purple gradients. Or any gradient. The palette is flat. Three solid colours, full stop.

NEVER

Inter, Geist, Söhne, Manrope, Space Grotesk. They're the AI typography defaults. The whole point of choosing Instrument + Hanken is to not be one of those sites.

NEVER

Nested cards inside cards. One layer of containment is plenty. Cardocalypse is over.

NEVER

Big rounded outline icons above headings. The puck is the visual anchor, not a Lucide icon library.

NEVER

Glassmorphism, neon glow, monospace-as-vibe. The mono is for system text, not for atmosphere.

NEVER

"Harness," "seamlessly," "intelligent," "powerful," "robust." The copy is specific or it's nothing.

NEVER

Selling cleverness. "Deal with it" tips into smug. The brand is confident, not aggressive.
